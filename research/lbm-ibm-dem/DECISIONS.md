# Development record and evidence boundaries

This is the technical record of the project conversation, not a verbatim transcript or a record of unrelated account activity. Reported timings below are archival single-CPU measurements, not new speed measurements from the Git import.

| Stage | Request and implementation | Result and decision |
|---|---|---|
| Application review | Identify direct microplastic LBM studies, related LBM–DEM methods, researchers, codes, limitations and applications. Separate particle tracking, resolved IBM, continuum attachment and true contact mechanics. | Use saturated constrictions and bead packs as eventual experimental targets. Validate spatial retention, permeability change and release, not only an outlet breakthrough curve. |
| Method requirements | Two-way fluid–body exchange, frictional history and physical stiffness; lower total cost at equal accuracy. Explicitly include IBM. | AMR, GPU acceleration, implicit IBM, cached blocks and subcycling already have prior art. Contact-sensitive error control and reuse are research hypotheses, not verified novelty. A recycled fixed-skeleton Stokes operator is an alternative only when fluid inertia/transients can be neglected. |
| v0.1 | D2Q9 TRT; matched marker interpolation/spreading; global implicit two-way constraint; fictitious-interior linear/angular impulse; compliant frictional disks. Compare Jacobi and structured cached block preconditioning. | Archived 35 verification groups. Median speed ratios 1.110, 1.095 and 1.142 for separated disks, nearby disks and a fixed bed plus a mobile disk. No contacts in the timing workloads. Lower iteration count did not translate into proportional runtime reduction. |
| v0.2 | Add positive-core-gap wet normal/oblique encounters, a mechanical asperity envelope, prescribed normal/sliding/rotational resistance probes and optional three-mode cylinder near-field resistance. | Archived 55 test groups, 21 wet-impact cases and 24 probes. Median reference/fast 1.15 normal and 1.04 oblique, with overlapping timing ranges. In a normal case 8 to 16 DEM substeps changed sampled peak force by 0.00172%, versus 7.95% for a spatial comparison and 19.97% for the full fixed-grid fluid-timestep range. Samples are not guaranteed continuous peaks. |
| Near-gap decision | At h/a=1/8, resolved normal resistance/viscosity without retraction was 307.10, 206.41, 163.82 at 8,16,32 cells/radius; with 0.3-cell retraction: 155.56, 141.42, 133.19. | Coarse IBM can already overpredict resistance. A positive added drag cannot correct excess resistance. Keep the additional term off by default until matched; do not use a leading asymptote as an exact finite-domain reference. |
| v0.3: active update | Compare full-grid and exact marker-support population impulse commits at the same equations, tolerances and timestep. | Normal impact, 5 pairs per scheme: split 1.165195 to 1.016313 s (12.8% saving); iterated 2.400803 to 2.129220 s (11.3%). All ten paired final population/body dumps byte-identical. No external executable or GPU comparison. |
| v0.3: exchange selection | Iterated hydro/contact exchange restores populations, bodies and tangential history before each new trial. An initial powers-of-two sweep favoured iteration at the stated admissibility gate. | A post hoc intermediate-step check reversed the ranking. At 960 release steps both schemes passed in both orientations. Split/iterated medians: normal 4.0037/7.0388 s; oblique 3.9570/5.9672 s; 3 pairs per orientation. **Recommendation: split + active** on these cases, not a universal 960-step rule. |
| v0.3: film | Standalone finite two-port Reynolds element for pressure-driven flow, sliding and squeezing; independent stationary Stokes comparison. | Conductance excess was 1.1732% at h/a=0.04 and 0.01180% at 0.000625. A separate prescribed-motion test found 10.9% less normal resistance at the base timestep than the finest. Standalone film is not integrated with moving IBM. |
| Further research | Request to investigate a fastest new IBM-inclusive solver. | No finished subsequent release or external speed benchmark was part of the sourced discussion. Retain v0.3 as the latest verified snapshot in this import. |

## Criteria for a faster solver paper

1. Fix governing equations, density/inertia, contact stiffness, frictional history, boundary conditions, physical time and shape. Publish hardware, precision and initialization.
2. Check fluid mass, linear/angular impulse, port power and contact dissipation; use independent references for force, torque, orientation, flow rate and pressure drop.
3. Select tolerances before timing and probe intermediate timesteps. Report failures, rejected trials, initialization, geometry rebuild, solve, communication, memory and total time. Compare internal variants and optimized external solvers only where physics match.
4. Move from single wet impacts to one pressure-driven throat, a two-particle bridge and then a 3D grain pack. Calibrate polymer properties and test held-out release/retention experiments before making environmental predictions.
